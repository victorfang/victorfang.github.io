How do the rocket controls work?

Play in landscape orientation. Choose a city and ramp, then tap Fly. After the countdown, use the two controls to balance your car in the air.

Left · Nose ↑

Fire the rear rockets to lift the nose.

Right · Nose ↓

Fire the front rockets to lower the nose.

Use short taps to save fuel. Both controls provide lift while helping you keep a steady angle. Aim to land wheels-down on the target pad. Open Teslaro 3D Menu → Tips to revisit the instructions. The menu also has Cars, Cities, Ramps and Settings.

First two ramps: Beginner landing assist is on by default. Hold both controls to level the car and manage landing thrust. It helps every car, including the stronger paid models, without changing their maximum performance. A gentle rear-wheel touch while rolling off the crest no longer ends the flight; body and hard impacts still count as crashes.

On a keyboard: Space launches, A or ← fires the rear rockets, D or → fires the front rockets, and R retries.

Which ramp should I try next?

Each of the 11 cities opens with two short beginner lessons: Mellow has a 12 m ramp and Crescent has a 16 m ramp. The next four courses have longer 32–82 m approaches. The ramp picker shows each approach length, the clear horizontal gap from takeoff to the near edge of the landing pad, and an Advanced or Expert label.

The 1×, 2× and 3× rocket recommendations are guides, and every car may attempt every course. A recommendation does not lock a ramp, grant a paid car or start a purchase. Aero and the other free cars provide the 1× baseline; Ice Blue provides 2× power and Midnight Gold provides 3× power. Dubai Gold and Platinum X provide 5× power. On Advanced and Expert courses, use shorter, carefully timed taps; these courses stay fully manual.

Look ahead before launching. The Golden Crown has an 82 m ramp, and its landing pad starts 76 m beyond the crest. Aim for that separate forward pad. On long Expert flights with 3× rockets, steady the car early, coast between bursts and keep fuel for a later lift burst to soften the descent. Holding the rockets throughout the climb can use the fuel you need for touchdown.

Car tiers, purchases and restoration

The garage has 12 original cars, arranged from free coupes to premium rocket racers. All performance comparisons use the free 1× baseline.

  • Free · 1×: Aero, Comet, Orbit, Miami Vice and Paris Éclair. Aero is the starting car. All five have identical rocket boost, fuel capacity, steering power and stabilization assistance, with different body, roof, paint and trim features.
  • Entry · $1 USD · 2×: Ice Blue, the cyan-accented roadster. All four performance values are twice the free baseline.
  • Mid · $5 USD each · 3×: Midnight Gold, Tokyo Neon, Singapore Ion and Austin Longhorn. Each has three times the free baseline for all four values.
  • Premium · $9 USD each · 5×: Dubai Gold and Platinum X. Both have five times the free baseline for all four values. Platinum X adds a metallic platinum finish and violet aero blades.

Stronger rockets accelerate the car harder during a burn, and larger tanks allow longer burns at the same input. The listed multipliers describe boost, fuel, steering and assist; they do not promise the same multiple of speed or flight time. Course geometry and landing rules are shared. On manual Advanced and Expert courses, higher-power cars, especially the 5× models, reward short taps and careful timing.

Each paid car is a separate optional one-time, non-consumable upgrade. The prices above are USD base prices; Apple shows your current localized price before you confirm when the product is available. In the native iPhone or iPad app, open Cars, select a car and use its purchase button. Opening the garage or previewing a car does not buy it. A purchase unlocks only that car and does not remove ads.

Already own Midnight Gold or Dubai Gold? Your existing verified purchase keeps access to that same car under the updated lineup. You do not need to buy it again. Use Restore purchases in the garage with the same Apple Account if it appears locked, including after reinstalling or on another supported device. Apple must verify ownership. Restoration does not recover local stars, game points or banked fuel, and a refunded or revoked purchase loses access.

Apple handles billing and refund requests. There is no recurring subscription. Purchases in TestFlight follow Apple’s test-purchase rules and are separate from production ownership.

Why is a car locked or checkout unavailable?

The browser supports locked premium-car previews and cannot complete Apple purchases. On iPhone or iPad, a purchase can be unavailable if the product is not offered yet, Apple cannot load it, connectivity is unavailable, or purchases are restricted on the device. Check your connection, reopen the app and try again. Do not buy repeatedly to resolve a delivery problem; try Restore purchases first if you already own that car.

All seven paid cars have separate Apple products. A lock means the selected car has no verified current entitlement. Buying one car does not unlock any other car. If you already own the selected car, restore with the same Apple Account and wait for Apple to verify it. Product availability depends on the App Store configuration and review status; a displayed price alone does not establish ownership. If a saved paid-car choice is unavailable, the game uses Aero while your verified purchases are restored.

Can I play offline? Where is my progress?

The installed iPhone and iPad game bundles its gameplay resources and works offline. Ads, App Store transactions and external links need an internet connection. No game account is required.

Selected cars, cities, ramps, stars, your game-points total, a banked next-flight fuel bonus and settings stay on your device. Browser progress is separate from native app progress. There is no cloud-save or cross-device progress service. Redesigned courses earn a fresh set of stars; previous course records remain stored on your device. Clearing website data or deleting the app can erase local progress; device backups may restore data according to your operating-system settings. Keep this in mind before reinstalling to troubleshoot.

Ads and privacy choices

The native iPhone and iPad app has no banner ads. Paced full-screen interstitials may appear only after a successfully completed ramp, when you tap a report action to continue. Failed flights do not create an interstitial opportunity. Interstitial pacing requires at least three successful ramp completions since the last full-screen ad and a 120-second interval from session start or the previous full-screen ad. Watching an optional rewarded video also restarts that interval and completion count.

Ads depend on connectivity, consent status and inventory. An unavailable ad does not delay the next flight or block core gameplay. The browser game does not request native AdMob ads.

Required advertising privacy messages are handled through Google UMP. When available for an ad-supported player, open Teslaro 3D Menu → Settings → Ad privacy to revisit privacy choices. Read our privacy policy for information about advertising providers, purchases, local game data and support messages.

Optional videos, game points and bonus fuel

When a video is available, you may choose Watch video · +100 points +25% fuel from the menu, flight report or Settings → Ads & points. A matching native SDK-earned result awards 100 game points and banks 25% extra fuel for your next flight. Skipping, dismissing before earning or failing to load a video does not grant the reward. Normal Play and Retry remain available without watching.

Only one fuel bonus can be banked at a time; it cannot stack. It survives menu, city and car changes, then is spent when the next flight actually begins after the countdown. The bonus extends available fuel without changing your selected car or rocket power.

Points and the banked fuel bonus are local, noncash game progress. Points cannot be exchanged for money, paid cars or Remove all ads. A video never grants StoreKit ownership. You can see your points in Settings; deleting local game data can erase points and unused fuel bonuses.

Remove all ads — optional one-time purchase

Open Teslaro 3D Menu → Settings → Ads & points to find Remove all ads. It is a separate optional, non-consumable Apple purchase with a $10 USD base price. Apple shows the current localized price before confirmation when the product is available. There is no recurring subscription.

After Apple verifies current ownership, the app stops new ad requests, suppresses interstitials and removes optional rewarded-video offers as well. A full-screen ad already presented finishes its normal dismissal flow. Previously earned game points and banked fuel remain available. Removing ads does not unlock any car or grant new video rewards.

Use Restore purchases in Settings or Cars with the same Apple Account after reinstalling or on another supported device. The app waits for verified entitlement information before requesting ads. A refunded or revoked Remove all ads purchase returns the app to the normal ad-supported rules once Apple verifies that change. The seven paid-car products remain separate: buying a car does not remove ads, and removing ads does not unlock a car.

If the price or buy button is unavailable, check your connection and device purchase restrictions and try again later. Do not buy again to fix a missing entitlement; restore first. Apple handles billing and refund requests. Restore purchases restores verified products, not local stars, points or unused fuel bonuses.

Settings, audio and display

Open Teslaro 3D Menu → Settings for audio, graphics, vibration and credits. Use the separate Music and Effects switches; both choices are remembered. The speaker button controls sound effects. Check your device’s media volume and tap once to allow audio playback. Music loops across menus, ramps and results: the default Hurricane Hero groove changes to the Middle East track in Dubai and the Asia track in Tokyo or Singapore, then returns to the default elsewhere. Completing a ramp plays the winning sound when effects are on.

Backgrounding, interruptions, full-screen ads and consent forms suspend audio; returning respects your settings.

Under Flight, Beginner landing assist is on by default for Mellow and Crescent, the first two ramps in each city. Hold both buttons for assisted landing thrust. Turn it off for manual flight. Single-button taps and all Advanced and Expert ramps use manual power regardless of this switch. Your choice is remembered.

Under Graphics, choose High for richer lighting or Balanced for a lighter graphics mode. Turn on Reduce motion to reduce motion and animated effects. The Vibration switch controls feedback on supported iPhones. These preferences stay on your device.

Turn your iPhone or iPad sideways to play. The portrait screen pauses the game and asks you to rotate. Use Back to menu in Settings when you are finished, then Play to return to the game.

How do I save or share a flight report?

After a flight, open Share on the flight report. The report includes snapshots from your actual run. Choose the system share option or Save PNG. Available destinations depend on your device, browser and installed apps.

X and Facebook options open a link composer; they do not automatically attach the report image. To share the image manually, save it first and attach it in your chosen app. The game does not publish a post without your action.

If something still goes wrong, email Fang Ventures with your device, app version and the steps that led to the problem.
